# Sermon for the 6th Sunday of Easter, Year A - 2026
**Fr. Christoph Kreitmeir**

*(Readings: Acts 8:5-8,14-17; Gospel: John 14:15-21)*

---
## 🌟 Core Message
Each of us knows the feeling of abandonment or the loss of a loved one. Sooner or later, we all face the reality that someone we were close to leaves us through death. The pain of loss is felt both emotionally and physically; we remember shared moments and try to relive them. How comforting it is then to be able to reconnect with the shared spirit that defined the relationship. The departed becomes present again, and the shared experiences continue to shape the life of those left behind.

The same was true for Jesus and His disciples. Shortly before His death, Jesus assured His friends that He would not leave them as orphans, because the common Father in heaven - God - would send them a Helper who would always remain with them. The Greek word "Parakletos" means not only Helper but also Comforter, Advocate, Counselor, and Encourager - someone called to one's side to assist.

This enduring power is the Holy Spirit, which we Christians receive in Baptism and Confirmation. Often, He is buried, banished to the farthest corner of the soul, or simply forgotten. Yet, He remains the battery to which we only need to connect to live well, righteously, truthfully, and uprightly. The spirit by which someone lives becomes evident over time through their words and actions.

## 💡 Key Points
- The Holy Spirit is the Helper promised by Jesus to His disciples - a Comforter, Advocate, and Encourager.
- The fruits of the Holy Spirit are: a life of order and truth, love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (cf. Gal 5:22-23).
- The Spirit of God works in those who believe in Jesus and become His disciples. His effect is seen in the transformation of people and their positive character traits.
- Even today, there are people who reconnect with the power of the Holy Spirit, turning their lives around: from resignation to courageous action, from negative criticism to joy.

## ❓ How Do I Stay in Contact with Jesus?
Maintaining contact with Jesus is often understood as building a personal relationship nurtured through communication, trust, and shared time. Here are practical ways to strengthen this connection in daily life:

### 1. Prayer as Dialogue
- Openness: Talk to Jesus as you would with a good friend - about joy, worries, doubts, and gratitude.
- Quiet Time: Set aside regular times in your day to pray in solitude, such as in the morning or evening.
- Listening: Prayer is not just about speaking; it's also about creating space for God to respond in your heart.

### 2. Reading the Bible for Guidance
- Scripture Study: Reading the Bible, especially the Gospels, helps you get to know Jesus better.
- Daily Inspiration: Regular reading of Scripture strengthens faith and opens you to spiritual insights.

### 3. Integration into Daily Life
- Mental Exchange: Consciously include Jesus in your daily routine by directing brief thoughts to Him throughout the day.
- Learning Trust: Ask for guidance and rely on His promises.

### 4. Community and Fellowship
- Contact with Other Christians: Sharing with fellow believers can deepen your relationship with Jesus.
- Worship and Small Groups: Attending services or small groups (house churches) helps experience community.

### 5. Open Heart
- Willingness: Actively seek encounter, for the willingness of the heart is the most important place of meeting.
- Perseverance: It's normal for the intensity of the relationship to change over time. Stay connected, even in dry periods.
